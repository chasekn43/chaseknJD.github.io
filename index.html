Executive Case Summary

This repository serves as a permanent, public evidentiary record documenting systemic compliance failures, account lockdowns, and false regulatory disclosures by Affirm, Inc. (NYSE: AFRM). Prepared by a dual-credentialed Attorney and CPA, this case study details how an automated BNPL credit engine processed an unauthorized fraud loan, locked out user interface payment controls for performing loans, and submitted material false statements to federal regulators claiming a non-existent consumer chargeback.

Key Case Milestones & Fully Documented Paper Trail

  • July 7, 2026 (11:53 AM CDT): Unauthorized fraudulent transaction PE270138 ($104.63) originated on Shop Pay / Affirm, shipping perfume to San Jose, CA. [Supported by Doc #3]
  • July 7, 2026 (12:55 PM CDT & Follow-Up) — Telephonic Fraud Alerts: Consumer placed emergency 20-minute call to Affirm Customer Support immediately after fraud hit. Frontline agents told consumer to "rest assured" they would not be charged, refused an administrative freeze due to "pending" status, and instructed consumer to call back after status changed. A follow-up call was also placed to reinforce the fraud report. [Supported by Doc #2]
  • July 7, 2026 (1:27 PM – 4:12 PM CDT) — The Loading Dock Window: Shipping label created in Farmers Branch, TX (75234) at 1:27 PM, but package did not receive carrier origin scan in DeSoto, TX (75115) until 4:12 PM — giving Affirm and merchant 2 hours 45 minutes to pull box off dock. [Supported by Doc #3]
  • July 9, 2026 (11:47 AM CDT): Affirm Customer Care (Agent "Jason") dispatched formal email promising a 30-day investigation and credit hold. [Supported by Doc #6]
  • July 9, 2026 (12:21 PM & 2:15 PM CDT) — Supplemental Investigation Calls: Consumer placed two supplemental calls totaling 56 minutes to Affirm Customer Support to provide carrier tracking metrics, police report details, and evidence for the active investigation file. [Supported by Doc #2]
  • July 9, 2026 (1:13 PM CDT — The 86-Minute Sham Denial): Exactly 86 minutes after Agent "Jason's" email, Affirm's automated credit engine dispatched a final denial closing the investigation without human review of IP logs, device fingerprints, or geographic anomalies. [Supported by Doc #6]
