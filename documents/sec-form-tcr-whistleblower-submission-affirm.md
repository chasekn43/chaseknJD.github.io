### PART II: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY OF SECURITIES VIOLATIONS

This Whistleblower submission provides primary-source, certified documentation establishing that **Affirm Holdings, Inc. (NASDAQ: AFRM)** has engaged in systemic securities violations and internal control failures, specifically:

1. **Falsification of Regulatory Filings (18 U.S.C. § 1001)**:
   In an official written disclosure submitted to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB Complaint #260717-35668593), Affirm fabricated an external "$26.16 consumer bank chargeback" to explain away an uncredited loan balance caused by internal ledger friction. Certified bank ACH clearing records establish that no consumer chargeback was ever initiated.

2. **Sarbanes-Oxley (SOX) Internal Control Deficiencies (§§ 302 & 404)**:
   Affirm’s automated transaction clearing systems cannot reconcile internal virtual card merchant adjustments with outstanding installment balances. To prevent internal ledger breaks from becoming visible on financial statements, Affirm’s software automatically locks consumer accounts and suppresses dispute logging.

3. **Concealment of Non-Conforming Debt in Warehouse Credit Facilities**:
   Affirm pledges consumer receivables to revolving credit facilities and Asset-Backed Securitization (ABS) structures (funded by Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, and Barclays). Affirm’s credit agreements require pledged collateral to be free of active consumer disputes and legal claims. By algorithmically rejecting disputes in 86 minutes and misreporting balances, Affirm unlawfully preserves the borrowing base eligibility of non-conforming debt.

4. **Under-Provisioning of Credit Loss Reserves (CECL)**:
   By misclassifying uncollectible and disputed receivables as external chargebacks or active performing loans, Affirm artificially depresses its Allowance for Credit Losses under Current Expected Credit Losses (CECL) accounting standards, misleading public market investors.

### PART III: CHRONOLOGICAL STATEMENT OF FACTS

* **July 7, 2026**: An unauthorized merchant transaction ($285.85) was generated across the country on an Affirm single-use virtual card. Carrier delivery tracking (OnTrac) confirmed the item was delivered to an unauthorized address in California, contradicting the account holder's registered residence in Monroe, Louisiana.
* **July 10, 2026**: Account holder filed a formal criminal incident report with the Monroe Police Department (Incident Report #26-29572) and submitted a dispute notice to Affirm.
* **July 10, 2026 (86 Minutes Later)**: Affirm’s automated machine-learning dispute bot issued an automated denial of the fraud claim, failing to review police reports or carrier tracking.
* **July 16, 2026**: Following executive escalation, Affirm issued written clearance confirming the transaction was unauthorized and stating: *"You will not be held responsible for the disputed balance."*
* **July 17, 2026**: Despite acknowledging the fraud in writing, Affirm’s internal managing counsel disabled in-app payment rails for the consumer's legitimate, performing loans while automated billing engines continued issuing collection notices.
* **July 22, 2026**: In response to CFPB Complaint #260717-35668593, Affirm submitted a formal written response asserting that an internal refund could not be credited due to an *"external $26.16 bank chargeback."* Official bank ACH clearing logs confirm zero bank chargebacks were ever filed.
* **August 5, 2026**: Outside counsel (Morgan Lewis partner Arjun Rao) intervened to threaten collection actions, which collapsed when the consumer produced direct bank BillPay ACH clearing traces proving all legitimate debt was current.
* **August 11, 2026**: The California Department of Justice (Public Inquiry Unit File #1553638 / Law Enforcement Docket #26-1884) formally classified Affirm's complaint records as confidential law enforcement investigatory files.
* **August 14, 2026**: A formal State Bar of California Attorney Misconduct Complaint was filed against Morgan Lewis partner Arjun Rao (CA Bar #265347) for ratifying false regulatory disclosures.

### PART IV: STATUTORY CAUSES OF ACTION & SECURITIES VIOLATIONS

#### COUNT I: Material Misstatements in Regulatory Filings (18 U.S.C. § 1001 & Exchange Act § 10(b))
Affirm knowingly submitted false factual statements to a federal regulatory agency (CFPB) to disguise an internal accounting error. Mischaracterizing internal clearing discrepancies as consumer-initiated bank chargebacks constitutes an intentional deception to avoid regulatory penalties and audit scrutiny.

#### COUNT II: Ineffective Internal Accounting Controls (SOX §§ 302 & 404, 15 U.S.C. § 7241/7262)
Affirm's CEO and CFO certify the effectiveness of internal controls in quarterly SEC Form 10-Q filings. The documented inability of Affirm’s ledger to credit merchant adjustments without locking performing consumer lines represents a material weakness in internal controls over transaction processing.

#### COUNT III: Misrepresentations in ABS & Warehouse Borrowing Base Disclosures (Form ABS-EE)
Under Regulation AB, issuers must disclose repurchase demands and covenant breaches on securitized loans. By using automated bots to suppress dispute logging, Affirm avoids triggering mandatory loan repurchase clauses in its credit facilities with Goldman Sachs, Barclays, and Morgan Stanley.
